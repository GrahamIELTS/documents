You Write Longer Sentences Than the Band 9s

You told me you do not write long enough sentences. I measured your two scripts against 400 pieces of writing from my other students and against 458 Band 9 scoring essays. The belief is not true, and holding it is costing you the thing that would actually raise your score.

Part 01The claim, tested

Here is every sentence in your Task 2, drawn to scale. The two lines mark the averages you are being compared with.

Your Task 2 sentences, in the order you wrote them

Each bar is one sentence. Length in words.

Your average is 23.3 words. My other students average 19.6 and the Band 9 essays average 20.3. Your longest sentence is 45 words; the Band 9 essays average 34.6 for their longest.

What this means

Among my other students your Task 2 sentence length sits at the 85th percentile. Only fifteen students in a hundred write longer sentences than you do. Your clauses per sentence sit at the 86th percentile, and the amount of detail you carry in clauses sits at the 92nd.

You are not a student who writes short, simple sentences. You are a student who writes long, clause-heavy ones, and you are doing it more than the Band 9 writers do.

If you take one thing from this report, take this. Every hour you spend trying to make your sentences longer is an hour spent making the one thing you already overdo worse.

Part 06Your Task 1 sentences that do not finish

Your Task 1 runs at 0.92 clauses per sentence. Of the 400 scripts I measured, that is the lowest. A number below one means some of your sentences contain no working verb at all.

Why this matters more than it looks

This is why your Task 1 sentence length reads as very short. It is not that you are writing simple sentences. It is that some of what you have punctuated as a sentence is a noun phrase with a full stop attached. An examiner reads that as a grammar error, not as a style choice, and it sits in the same criterion that your excellent Task 1 vocabulary is helping.

Fix these three and your Task 1 becomes a genuinely strong script, because everything else in it is already at or above the Band 9 average.

Part 07The rewrite that changes your Task 2

One habit, four times per essay. This is Part 05, item two, made concrete.

Take any sentence of yours with because, which, so that, that is why, or a comma joining two ideas. Delete the joining word and turn the second half into a noun group.

What you wrote
  • As technology continues to advance, it's convenient for people during work or learning new knowledge, which in turn leads to people relying on using computers or AI tools to acquire information.31 words, four clauses
  • In a traditional classroom, students could only get what was shown on books, whereas digital tools make information easier to absorb and remember.23 words, three clauses
The same idea, compressed
  • Rapid technological advance has made information gathering convenient at work and in study, and that convenience breeds dependence on computers and AI tools.23 words, two clauses, three noun groups doing the work
  • Traditional classrooms limited students to the contents of a textbook, whereas digital tools make information easier to absorb.18 words, two clauses

Both rewrites are shorter, both carry the same meaning, and both are graded higher, because the detail has moved from extra clauses into the noun phrases. That is the difference the Band 9 essays show over yours, and it is the only structural difference that matters.

Part 08What to do next

  1. Put a space after every full stop and comma

    Nine of them are missing in your Task 2 and it takes your visible word count under the minimum. Do this before anything else on this page.

  2. Do the noun-phrase rewrite four times per Task 2 essay

    Once per paragraph. Use Part 07 as the model. This is the largest gap between your writing and Band 9.

  3. Write your Task 2 as though you were describing a chart

    Before you start arguing, remind yourself of the register you used for significant, dramatically, inconsistent. Same care, different task.

  4. Read each Task 1 sentence and find the main verb

    If you cannot point at one, the sentence is not finished. Three of your eleven failed this test.

  5. Add about 35 words to Task 2, as detail not as sentences

    You are at 256 and the target is around 290. Extend the examples you already have rather than adding new points.

  6. Stop trying to write longer sentences

    You are already above the Band 9 average. Leave sentence length alone entirely and spend that effort on items two and three.

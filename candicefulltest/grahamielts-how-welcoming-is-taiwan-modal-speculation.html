Engoo Daily News · 11 August 2026

These Are the World’s Most “Welcoming” Cities

Level 6 · Intermediate · supplied lesson text

Singapore is the world’s most “welcoming” city according to a new ranking that gives cities scores on things like English language ability and safety — as well as friendliness.

To find out where the world’s most welcoming cities are, an eSIM company called Holafly looked at 200 cities around the world that included 100 of the world’s most-visited cities and 100 cities with the biggest populations.

Researchers at Holafly collected data on six different ways in which cities can be welcoming to tourists, giving scores for each. These included scores based on safety, English language ability, the number of tourism and information services, how easy it is to visit without a visa, how easy it is to get help if you’re in trouble, and how often people say the city is “friendly” or “welcoming” online.

Taking first place in the ranking, Singapore scored very well for safety, for English language ability, and for how easy it is to visit without a visa. The city also has a good number of tourism and information services to make life easier for first-time visitors.

London in the UK took second place, and not surprisingly scored very well for English language ability! London also got a good score for its tourism and information services, and a high number of mentions online as a “friendly” or “welcoming” city!

In third place was Sydney in Australia, which also scored well for English language ability, tourism and information services, and had a fairly high score for safety.

Holafly also made separate rankings for the world’s most helpful cities and safest cities.

It said the world’s most helpful city is Tokyo in Japan, followed by Hamburg in Germany and Birmingham in the UK. And the world’s safest city is Sapporo in Japan, followed by Abu Dhabi in the UAE, and Yokohama in Japan.

Stage 5 · Teach

Match the modal to the evidence

42–54 minutes

Now compare your Test 1 choices with the system below. The key decision is not “Which phrase sounds advanced?” It is “How strongly does my evidence support this conclusion?”

could be

A possible explanation or result. The evidence leaves several options open.

Different methods could be producing different winners.

might be

A real but uncertain possibility. Often interchangeable with could be here.

Taiwan might be high in a country ranking.

must be

A strong logical conclusion from evidence—not an obligation and not absolute proof.

Taipei must be strong on safety in this data set.

open possibilityuncertainstrong deduction
evidence + subject + modal + base verb + reason

Taipei is #7 for safety, so it must be performing strongly on that measure.

Form

Use the base form: must be, not must to be or must is. No third-person -s follows a modal.

Meaning

Must be means “the evidence strongly points to this.” Do not use it to generalise from a city to every person or place.

Could vs might

Both are acceptable for an uncertain possibility in today’s tasks. Do not force a fixed percentage difference.

Pronunciation

Link the phrase smoothly: /kʊd biː/, /maɪt biː/, /mʌs(t) biː/. The /t/ in must may be light in connected speech.

Form surgery

1

Which form is accurate?

2

Which form expresses a possibility?

3

Which form keeps the main verb in the base form?

Board this contrast: “The ranking says Taipei is #7 for safety → it must be strong on safety.” / “It gives no English score → English might be / could be a weaker factor.” Ask the student to explain why must is not suitable in the second sentence.

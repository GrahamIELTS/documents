Your writing is closer than your score says

Two scripts, measured line by line against my other students who answered these exact two questions, and against band 9 scoring essays. One habit is holding the rest of it down, and it is a habit you can break in a week.

Your other skills came out well ahead of your writing. That gap is worth explaining, because it usually means one of two things: either the writing is genuinely weaker, or something narrow and fixable is capping it. In your case it is the second one, and I can show you exactly where.

I compared your two scripts against 35 students who answered this same graph and 76 who answered this same computers question, plus a set of band 9 scoring essays measured the same way. Same task, same conditions, so the comparison is fair rather than general.

Part two

The one habit holding your score down

You write very long sentences. Not sometimes. In both tasks, consistently, and further past everyone else than any other measure in your writing.

Why this costs you marks, and it is not the reason you think

Long sentences are not penalised for being long. They cost you because of what happens inside them. Look at where your mistakes actually sit:

You wroteIn contrast, the lowest percentage were Indonesia from 1970 to 1990, and Thailand from 2010 to 2040.

FixedIndonesia had the lowest percentage from 1970 to 1990. Thailand was lowest from 2010 onwards.

You wroteMost of the countries steadily rose over the years, reaching their peak in 2040, with the highest, Malaysia reaching a percentage of a little over 80% and the lowest, Thailand climbing up to almost 50%. (35 words)

FixedThree of the four countries rose steadily and peaked in 2040. Malaysia finished highest at 83%. Thailand finished lowest at 50%. (three sentences, 21 words)

You wroteHealth problems being one of them, as staring at screens for a long period of time can have a bad impact on our eyesight, using computers to study can also cause that.

FixedHealth is one of them. Staring at a screen for hours damages eyesight, and studying on a computer does exactly that.

In every case the sentence started with a clear subject and lost it somewhere in the middle. The grammar did not fail because you do not know the grammar. It failed because by word 30 you were no longer holding the subject you started with. Shorter sentences would have made all three of those errors impossible.

The instruction is the opposite of the usual one

Almost everyone is told to write longer, more complex sentences to sound advanced. You are already past band 9 on sentence length in both tasks. Writing longer would make your writing worse, and it is the one piece of advice you should ignore.

Split every sentence that runs past 25 words. That single change fixes your accuracy, raises your Task 1 word count, and forces new vocabulary, for reasons I will come to.

Part three

Task 1: two fixes worth a band between them

Your report is 150 words

The minimum is 150. You have no margin at all. If the examiner counts one thing differently from the way you counted it, you are under the limit, and an under-length Task 1 is penalised before a single sentence is judged on its quality.

Only 3 of the 35 students on this graph wrote fewer words than you. The usual answer is 193. Band 9 reports come in around 173. Aim for 175 to 185. Not 250: over-writing Task 1 costs you too, because selection is part of what is being marked. Between 170 and 190 is the safe room.

Here is the good news. You are short because you wrote five sentences, not because you had nothing to say. Split those five into nine and you will land near 180 without adding a single new idea.

You never actually give a number

You mention five figures. Every one of them is an approximation:

  • a little over 80%
  • almost 50%
  • a little below 50%
  • a few percent above 40%
  • little above 50%

The phrase a little appears three times in 150 words. The usual number of figures on this task is seven, and other students mix approximations with values read straight off the graph. You give the examiner no value to check you against.

This matters more than it looks. Task Achievement asks you to select and report key features accurately. An approximation of an approximation cannot be marked as accurate, because there is nothing in it to be accurate about.

You wrotedropped slightly in 2000 and 2010, going down from a little below 50% to a few percent above 40%

Write insteadfell from 49% in 1990 to 46% in 2010

Approximators are not the problem. About, just over and roughly are all standard Task 1 language and you should keep using them. The problem is that yours never arrive at a figure. About 83% is a reading. A little over 80% is a refusal to read.

Two small things while you are in there

  • cites should be cities. Worth thirty seconds of proofreading at the end.
  • You never say what the units are. One clause does it: Figures are given as a percentage of each country's total population. It is nearly free and it reads as control.
Part four

Task 2: it is repetition, not vocabulary

This is the finding I most want you to take away, because it changes what you should study.

In 318 words you write computers ten times, education seven times and a lot of four times. Efficiency and self control each carry a whole paragraph on their own.

Your vocabulary level is not the emergency

3.5% of your Task 2 words are at B2 or above. That is low, and it is worth raising, but 27 of the 76 students are lower than you and they are not all scoring below you. Meanwhile your variety puts you in the bottom tenth.

So the instruction is not "learn harder words". It is stop using the same word three times. You already know the alternatives. In Task 1 you reach for precise language without prompting, which proves the words are in there.

What to do about it, mechanically

Every time you need to name the thing again, name it differently. Not with a thesaurus word you are unsure of, with a phrase you already own:

There is a second reason this is urgent, and it links straight back to Part two. When you write one long sentence instead of three short ones, you only get one subject. Three sentences need three subjects, and three subjects need three different ways of naming the thing. Splitting your sentences will lift your word variety on its own, without you studying a single new word. That is why the sentence habit is the first fix and not the third.

Part five

Where Mandarin is showing through

Some of what looks like carelessness is not carelessness. It is your first language doing something English will not let it do. Once you can name the pattern you can check for it in the last two minutes of the exam, which is much easier than trying to feel it as you write.

Two of these are shared by a third of the students who answered this question, so they are predictable rather than personal. That is good news: predictable things can be drilled.

1. One word for one thing and many things

You wrote computers should be used in education, because even though it has a lot of problems, and then it can also hugely increase, and then it can be used to take notes. Every one of those should be they.

In Mandarin, 它 does not change for number, and nothing in the sentence forces you to decide. In English the pronoun has to agree with what it points back to, and the examiner sees a grammatical error rather than a style choice. 27 of the 76 students on this question did the same thing.

The check: find every it. Ask what it points at. If that thing ends in s, the pronoun is they.

2. Saying your opinion twice

In my opinion, I think that computers should be used in education. Both halves do the same job. 我認為 and 在我看來 stack comfortably in Mandarin; in English one cancels the other and the sentence reads as padding. 10 of the 76 did this.

Pick one. In my view, computers should be used in education. Or just Computers should be used in education.

3. Making a percentage into a country

the lowest percentage were Indonesia. In Mandarin, 最低的百分比是印尼 is perfectly natural, because the topic comes first and the comment attaches to it loosely. English insists that a percentage belongs to a country rather than being one.

Fix: Indonesia had the lowest percentage, or the lowest percentage was Indonesia's.

4. Countries that go up

Most of the countries steadily rose over the years. The countries did not rise. The share of their population living in cities rose. Mandarin lets the topic carry the comment, so 大部分國家穩定上升 is fine. English wants the thing that actually moved as the subject. 4 of the 35 students on this graph made the same swap.

Fix: The figure rose steadily in most of these countries.

5. Most and than in the same sentence

Malaysia has the most population living in cites than the other three countries. Two comparisons are fighting: 最 (most) and 比 (than). English makes you choose one shape.

Either Malaysia has the highest percentage of the four, or Malaysia has a higher percentage than the other three. Also, it is the percentage that is highest, not the population.

6. Plurals that do not get marked

with all kind of AI tools should be all kinds of. Mandarin does not require the plural to be marked when the quantity is already obvious from 各種. English marks it anyway, every time.

One that is not Mandarin, just register

it's, For one,, Another is that and search something up on line are all conversational. Write it is, Firstly, A second advantage is that and search online. This is a five minute fix and it lifts the whole tone of the essay.

Part six

What to do first, in order

These are ranked by how much score they return for the time they take. Do them in this order. The first one is worth more than the rest put together, because the others partly fall out of it.

  1. Split every sentence longer than 25 words

    Write as you do now, then go back and break anything long into two or three. Nothing else changes. This is the whole habit.

    It fixes your grammar errors, because they all live in the middle of long sentences. It takes your Task 1 from 150 words to about 180. It raises your word variety, because new sentences need new subjects. Three problems, one change.

  2. In Task 1, commit to a figure

    Every time you write a little, stop and read the value off the axis instead. Aim for six or seven figures, each one attached to a year.

  3. Count your Task 1 words before you leave the page

    Under 170 and you go back and add a detail sentence to a body paragraph. Never leave it at 150 again.

  4. Run the "it" check on Task 2

    Two minutes at the end. Every it, what does it point at, is that thing plural. This alone removes three errors from the script you gave me.

  5. Ban your third use of any content word

    When you are about to write computers for the third time, write something else. Same for a lot of. Keep a list of five alternatives for the topic words you know will come up.

  6. Then, and only then, work on vocabulary level

    Your Task 2 range is genuinely low and it will need to come up eventually. But it is fifth on this list, not first, and studying word lists while your sentences are still 30 words long will not move your score.

What I expect this to be worth

Nothing in this report asks you to learn anything new. Every fix is a check you run on writing you can already produce. You have the structure, you have the argument, you have the noun phrases, and in Task 1 you have the vocabulary. What you do not yet have is the habit of finishing a sentence before starting the next idea.

That is why your writing score sits behind the rest of you. It is a control problem, not a knowledge problem, and control is the fastest thing in this exam to fix.
